## 2. Data model plan

Add the following Mongoose models:

1. **Customer** — account owner, credit/walk-in category, company/contact details, GST and billing address, shipping addresses, credit terms/limit, assigned rate card, monthly billing configuration, and outstanding balance as required by Section 4.
2. **SavedPartyAddress** *(or embedded subdocuments on Customer, pending client volume/reuse decision)* — shipper/consignor, consignee, and pickup contacts with address, phone/email, GST where applicable, and customer association where relevant.
3. **CustomerRateCard** — walk-in standard card or customer-specific card; zone and weight slabs, Surface/Express prices, additional-weight charges, fuel/COD/other charges, GST treatment, and active/effective dates.
4. **CourierPartner** — enabled partner, adapter identifier, capabilities, and non-secret configuration references. Keep API secrets out of regular customer/shipment records.
5. **CourierRateQuote** — shipment/draft reference, partner and service, serviceability, ETA where available, normalized charges, total carrier quote, response reference, quote timestamp/expiry, and provider response metadata needed for support/reporting.

Modify **AppShipment** additively:

- Add an explicit pricing/selection snapshot (or typed optional subdocument) for chosen partner, selected quote, customer category/rate card version, selling charges/GST, and profit calculation inputs.
- Support an explicit `draft` lifecycle state while retaining current `creating` and `saved` meanings. Do not change the current default state for legacy creation.
- Preserve the existing unique `(owner, reference)` index and owner scoping. Existing `record` is `Mixed`; old records must remain readable without backfill and serializers must handle absent new fields.
- Keep reverse shipments (`kind: "reverse"`) out of new forward-draft lists and avoid changing historical records.

The new rate and quote snapshots must be **immutable for completed historical bookings**. Updating a customer’s current rate card must not recalculate older shipments.

- **Customer Management:** customer list/detail and create/edit form for credit/walk-in categories, company/contact, GST/billing/shipping addresses, credit settings, and saved shipper/consignee profiles.
- **Rate Master:** rate-card list and editor for customer or walk-in scope, zones, weight slabs, mode, surcharges, and GST settings.
- **Booking workflow:** extend the existing `CreateShipmentPage` and step components to match Appendix A sequence. Add customer selection/category, saved shipper/consignee pickers, same-as-shipper/new pickup selection, package category/handling, and partner-specific weight preview.
- **Rate comparison:** embed a comparison step/panel showing partner, modes, rate, ETA, serviceability, charge breakdown, total carrier cost, customer selling price, and estimated profit/margin. Operator selects a carrier/service explicitly.
- **Drafts/Pending AWB:** list and resume saved drafts, edit them, refresh expired quotes, and initiate AWB generation later.
- **Shipment details:** show selected carrier, AWB/reference, immutable pricing snapshot, and per-shipment profitability. Avoid building Phase 4 analytics or Phase 3 invoice/payment workflows as part of this Phase 1 plan.

## 4. Backend routes and services

Keep the active authenticated shipment path in [appShipmentRouter.js](D:/delhivery-backebnd/services/appShipmentRouter.js) as the foundation. The standalone [createShipmentController.js](D:/delhivery-backebnd/controller/createShipmentController.js) exists but is not the route used by the current app shipment flow.

Suggested route groups:

- `GET/POST /api/customers`, `GET/PATCH /api/customers/:id`, plus saved-party CRUD scoped to the authenticated owner.
- `GET/POST /api/rate-cards`, `GET/PATCH/DELETE /api/rate-cards/:id`, and an internal rate-resolution/calculation service.
- `GET /api/courier-partners` for enabled partners; management/configuration mechanism depends on the client’s operating model.
- `POST /api/shipping/quotes` to resolve customer selling price and request configured partner rates; persist each result, including partial failures.
- `POST /api/shipments/drafts`, `GET /api/shipments/drafts`, `GET/PATCH /api/shipments/drafts/:id`, and `POST /api/shipments/:id/book` for the AWB transition.

Use a provider contract for serviceability, quote, create-booking/AWB, and label retrieval. Adapt the current Delhivery controller paths through the first Delhivery adapter; avoid making the React UI provider-specific. Keep the legacy `/api/delhivery/...` paths and response shapes available during migration so existing Delhivery features and shipment history continue to work.

## 5. Quote, pricing, draft, and AWB workflows

1. **Build a comparable request:** customer/category, origin/destination, zone, product and package rows, chargeable-weight inputs, payment mode, COD amount, and shipment mode.
2. **Resolve customer selling price:** choose the applicable rate card and calculate the customer price, GST, and charges. Save the applied rate-card version and calculation inputs as a booking snapshot.
3. **Request partner quotes:** call each enabled adapter independently; normalize currency, serviceability, Surface/Express cost, ETA, and charge breakdown. Persist every success and failure/reference needed for quote reporting. A failed partner should not hide successful quotes from others.
4. **Manual selection:** display the comparison and require operator selection. Do not infer or automatically pick a “best” carrier.
5. **Save draft:** persist the editable booking and its pricing/quote references without making a manifestation/AWB request. On resume, preserve entered data and refresh quotes when expired.
6. **Generate AWB:** atomically reserve the booking transition, call only the selected partner, store its AWB and partner booking reference, label reference, final response metadata, and status. Keep existing duplicate-reference protection and uncertain-carrier-response safeguards.
7. **Show immutable economics:** retain original API quote, customer selling rate, and charge/GST snapshot. Track final partner invoice cost separately when its source becomes available; recalculate the shipment’s profitability record only through an explicit cost reconciliation action, never by changing the original quote or customer rate.

## 6. Unresolved client rules and external dependencies

These must be settled before finalizing calculations or partner adapters:

- How destination zones are defined and mapped from pincodes; whether zones differ by courier.
- Weight-slab boundaries, minimum/next slab behavior, rounding precision, volumetric divisor, and whether carrier-specific chargeable weight is per package or aggregated.
- Whether COD collection amount must equal order value; COD fee rules and taxes.
- Fuel/other surcharge calculation and GST rules, including what portion of partner GST is recoverable for the Section 8 profit formula.
- How customer selling rate and final invoice amount are rounded and displayed; treatment of discounts or other charges.
- Quote expiry and refresh behavior; handling of unavailable service, missing ETA, carrier timeouts, partial responses, or duplicate quote calls.
- Source/timing of **final partner invoice cost**: partner API, later invoice import, or authorized manual entry.
- Required shipper, consignee, pickup, product-category, GST, and special-handling fields per carrier.
- Which additional couriers are in scope, and their official API documentation, sandbox/base URLs, credentials, rate/serviceability/manifestation endpoints, authentication rules, response/error formats, and label behavior. Current Delhivery code demonstrates only the Delhivery integration; no other carrier capability or live endpoint behavior has been verified.

## 7. Milestones and testing checklist

1. **Requirements and data contract**
   - Confirm the business rules above and partner list/API documentation.
   - Define normalized booking, quote, rate-card, and pricing-snapshot shapes.
   - Record legacy response/data compatibility requirements.

2. **Customer, saved parties, and rate cards**
   - Add models, owner-scoped routes/controllers, validation, and list/edit screens.
   - Check credit and walk-in behavior, duplicate/customer isolation, saved-address reuse, and rate-card effective-date resolution.
   - Verify existing shipment records load unchanged.

3. **Pricing engine and courier adapters**
   - Implement pure customer-rate and weight calculations plus a normalized provider contract.
   - Use mocked/stubbed provider responses for comparison and partial-failure cases; do not call Delhivery in this phase.
   - Check zone/slab boundaries, dimensional weight, rounding, surcharges, GST, quote persistence, and quote expiry.

4. **Booking form and rate comparison**
   - Reorder sections to Appendix A and add customer/profile/rate fields.
   - Verify required field validation, same-as-shipper/new pickup behavior, package rows, COD handling, manual carrier selection, and displayed price breakdown.

5. **Draft/resume and AWB state transition**
   - Test draft save without carrier booking, resume/edit, quote refresh, and transition to booking.
   - Test duplicate submissions, concurrent requests, uncertain provider response, idempotent retry, and persisted AWB/partner reference.
   - Verify legacy shipments, existing Delhivery shipment actions, and reverse orders remain intact.

6. **Regression and acceptance**
   - Extend the existing backend account-shipment route tests and frontend API/workspace tests with mocked responses.
   - Confirm no legacy data migration is required—or define a reversible, additive migration if discovery shows one is necessary.
   - Obtain client acceptance of comparison fields, calculations, and booking screen sequence before any live carrier testing.
